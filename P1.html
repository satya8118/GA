1. A field with nine elements

Prove that \(x^2+1\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb F_3\). In \(K=\mathbb F_3[x]/(x^2+1)\), let \(u\) be the class of \(x\). List the elements of \(K\) and find \((1+u)^{-1}\).

Hint

Test the three elements of \(\mathbb F_3\). Multiply \(1+u\) by \(1-u\).

Solution

The values of \(x^2+1\) at \(0,1,2\) are \(1,2,2\), so the quadratic is irreducible and its quotient is a field. The nine elements are \(a+bu\) for \(a,b\in\{0,1,2\}\). Since \(u^2=-1\), \((1+u)(1-u)=2\). As \(2^{-1}=2\), the inverse is \(2(1-u)=2+u\).

2. Degrees in a tower

Let \(\alpha=\sqrt[4]{2}\) and \(E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\). Find the degrees and a basis at each step of \(\mathbb Q\subseteq E\subseteq \mathbb Q(\alpha)\).

Hint

Use Eisenstein at 2 for \(x^4-2\), then use \(\alpha^2=\sqrt2\).

Solution

Eisenstein at 2 shows \(x^4-2\) and \(x^2-2\) are irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\). Hence the degrees over \(\mathbb Q\) are 4 and 2, with bases \(1,\alpha,\alpha^2,\alpha^3\) and \(1,\sqrt2\), respectively. The tower law gives \([\mathbb Q(\alpha):E]=2\). Since \(x^2-\sqrt2\) annihilates \(\alpha\), it is the minimal polynomial over \(E\), and \(1,\alpha\) is an \(E\)-basis.

3. A sum of two radicals

Let \(s=\sqrt2+\sqrt3\). Prove that \(\mathbb Q(s)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)\) and find the minimal polynomial of \(s\).

Hint

Compute \(s^{-1}\) by multiplying \(s\) by \(\sqrt3-\sqrt2\).

Solution

The identity \(s^{-1}=\sqrt3-\sqrt2\) gives \(\sqrt3=(s+s^{-1})/2\) and \(\sqrt2=(s-s^{-1})/2\), proving equality of fields. If \(\sqrt3=a+b\sqrt2\) for \(a,b\in\mathbb Q\), squaring gives \(ab=0\). This would make either 3 or \(3/2\) a rational square, which is impossible because the exponent of 3 is odd. So \([\mathbb Q(s):\mathbb Q]=4\) by the tower law. From \(s^2=5+2\sqrt6\) we obtain \(s^4-10s^2+1=0\). Its degree is 4, so \(x^4-10x^2+1\) is the minimal polynomial.

4. Closure of the algebraic elements

Let \(K/F\) be any extension. Show that the elements of \(K\) algebraic over \(F\) form a subfield, and give an example where this subfield is proper.

Hint

For algebraic \(a,b\), first show that \(F(a,b)/F\) is finite.

Solution

Every element of \(F\) is algebraic, as witnessed by \(x-c\) for \(c\in F\). Both \(F(a)/F\) and \(F(a,b)/F(a)\) are finite, since a polynomial over \(F\) witnessing algebraicity of \(b\) also works over \(F(a)\). Their tower is finite, so every element of \(F(a,b)\), including \(a+b,ab,-a\), and \(a^{-1}\) when \(a\ne0\), is algebraic over \(F\). This proves the subfield claim. In \(F(t)\), the indeterminate \(t\) is transcendental over \(F\), so the subfield of algebraic elements is proper.

5. Algebraic of infinite degree

For \(n\ge1\), set \(E_n=\mathbb Q(2^{1/2^n})\), taking positive roots. Prove that \(E=\bigcup_{n\ge1}E_n\) is an algebraic field extension of infinite degree.

Hint

The generator of \(E_n\) is the square of the generator of \(E_{n+1}\). Apply Eisenstein at 2.

Solution

The squaring identity shows \(E_n\subseteq E_{n+1}\). Every pair of elements of the union belongs to a common stage, so sums, differences, products and inverses of nonzero elements remain in the union. Each element lies in a finite extension and is algebraic. Eisenstein gives \([E_n:\mathbb Q]=2^n\). Since each \(E_n\) is a \(\mathbb Q\)-vector subspace of \(E\) and these dimensions are unbounded, \(E\) has infinite degree.
