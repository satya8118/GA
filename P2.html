1. Separability in characteristic \(p\)

Let \(F=\mathbb F_p(t)\), with \(t\) an indeterminate, and let \(u^p=t\) in an algebraic closure. Show that \(x^p-t\) is irreducible over \(F\), find all its distinct roots, and determine the \(F\)-automorphisms of \(F(u)\).

Hint

Use Eisenstein in \(\mathbb F_p[t][x]\) and expand \((x-u)^p\).

Solution

By Eisenstein at the prime \(t\) and Gauss’s lemma, \(x^p-t\) is irreducible over \(F\). In characteristic \(p\), \(x^p-t=(x-u)^p\). Thus its only distinct root is \(u\), of multiplicity \(p\), so the polynomial is inseparable. Every \(F\)-automorphism sends \(u\) to a root and hence fixes \(u\). It therefore fixes all of \(F(u)\).

2. Embeddings and automorphisms

Put \(\alpha=\sqrt[3]{2}\), \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\subseteq\mathbb R\), and \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i/3}\). Find the three \(\mathbb Q\)-embeddings \(K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\). Which restrict to automorphisms of \(K\)?

Hint

The image of \(\alpha\) must be a root of \(x^3-2\). Use the quotient description to justify that each root gives an embedding.

Solution

Eisenstein at 2 gives minimal polynomial \(x^3-2\), with roots \(\alpha,\zeta\alpha,\zeta^2\alpha\). Each gives exactly one embedding by evaluating the quotient \(\mathbb Q[x]/(x^3-2)\) at that root. Only the real root \(\alpha\) lies in \(K\subseteq\mathbb R\), so only the identity embedding gives an automorphism of \(K\).

3. The splitting field of a cubic

With \(\alpha,\zeta\) as in Problem 2, find the splitting field \(L\) of \(x^3-2\), compute \([L:\mathbb Q]\), and identify its Galois group.

Hint

The ratio of two suitable roots is \(\zeta\). Over the real field \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), the polynomial \(x^2+x+1\) has no root.

Solution

The three roots lie in \(\mathbb Q(\alpha,\zeta)\), and their splitting field contains both \(\alpha\) and \(\zeta=(\zeta\alpha)/\alpha\). The tower degrees are 3 and 2, so the splitting field has degree 6. It is Galois because the polynomial splits there and is separable in characteristic zero. The group acts faithfully on its three roots, which generate \(L\). This gives an injective map into \(S_3\) between groups of order 6, hence the group is \(S_3\).

4. Which lengths are constructible?

Decide whether \(\sqrt{1+\sqrt2}\) and \(\sqrt[3]{2}\) are constructible from a unit segment. Justify both answers using field degrees or quadratic extensions.

Hint

Construct the first number in two square-root steps. For the second, use Eisenstein and the tower law.

Solution

First adjoin \(\sqrt2\), then take the positive square root of \(1+\sqrt2\). These steps give a tower of at most quadratic real extensions, so the first length is constructible. Eisenstein at 2 gives degree 3 for \(\sqrt[3]{2}\). Any constructible number lies in a field whose degree over \(\mathbb Q\) is a power of 2. Its own degree must divide that power by the tower law, which rules out 3.

5. An abelian extension inside a cyclotomic field

Let \(\zeta_8=e^{2\pi i/8}\). Show explicitly that \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\subseteq\mathbb Q(\zeta_8)\). List the automorphisms of \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_8)\) over \(\mathbb Q\) and identify which fix \(\sqrt2\).

Hint

Use \(\zeta_8+\zeta_8^{-1}=2\cos(\pi/4)\) and the odd residues modulo 8.

Solution

The sum \(\zeta_8+\zeta_8^{-1}\) equals \(\sqrt2\), giving the inclusion. The minimal polynomial is \(\Phi_8(x)=x^4+1\), and its four roots are \(\zeta_8^a\) for \(a=1,3,5,7\). Each root generates the same field because \(a\) is invertible modulo 8. Hence the automorphisms are \(\zeta_8\mapsto\zeta_8^a\) for those four \(a\). They send the sum to \(2\cos(a\pi/4)\), which is \(\sqrt2\) for \(a=1,7\) and \(-\sqrt2\) for \(a=3,5\).
